Each aspect of our project has some level of creativity due to the nature of what the project is. We are reviving the ScorBot ER-4pc and ER-V, legacy robotic arms that would otherwise have been discarded, and turned them into an autonomous camera platform. Several of our design decisions have creative solutions. 

One of our robot arms is missing a controller, so we are driving the motors directly using an esp32. This required writing custom firmware to accept the same commands as the previous controller. Because of this, we also have the capability to create our own commands, which extend beyond original capability of the robots. This will eventually be available on both robot arms. We are currently designing a custom PCB which would simplify our prototype.

We run pose recognition on a Raspberry Pi, which reduces latency in tracking. Our camera framing logic applies the rule of thirds, so the system composes shots the way a photographer would instead of just keeping a subject centered. We also built a digital twin, a virtual model of the system that lets us test and debug without risking the physical arms. The robots can also be controlled using a gamepad in addition to our user interface, making it more interactive and accessible.
